# Copywriting

Design gets attention; words make the decision. Every ngd site ships with real, specific, persuasive copy, written for the visitor, grounded in the keyword research, and in the voice chosen in discovery.

## 1. Before writing

- Re-read DESIGN.md: audience, the one primary action, differentiators, objections, tone, form of address.
- Collect the customer's own words: reviews (the business's and competitors'), forum threads and questions from the keyword research. Phrases customers use convert better than phrases businesses invent.
- Know each page's job and its primary keyword cluster (seo-plan.md) before writing it.

## 2. The five-second test

Within five seconds of landing, a visitor must be able to answer: **What is this? Is it for me? What do I do next?** The hero carries all three:
- **Headline:** the outcome or offer, for whom (not the company name, not a slogan that needs decoding).
- **Subheadline:** how it works or what makes it different, ideally with a specific detail or proof point.
- **Primary CTA:** a verb plus the value.
- **Proof nearby:** rating, client logos or a number, close to the CTA.

## 3. Headlines

- Lead with the benefit or outcome: "Pain-free running again — in 6 sessions or less" beats "Expert physiotherapy services".
- Be specific: numbers, timeframes, places, audiences. Specific sounds true.
- Short: 4–10 words for hero headlines; section headlines say the benefit of that section ("Book in 30 seconds", not "Booking").
- Useful patterns:
  - *[Outcome] for [audience]*: "Bookkeeping that runs itself, for freelancers"
  - *[Outcome] without [pain]*: "A spotless office without managing cleaners"
  - *The [category] for people who [desire]*: "The coffee subscription for people who care where it comes from"
  - A question only if the answer is obviously "yes" for the target visitor.
- Avoid: "Welcome to…", "We are…", vague superlatives ("innovative solutions"), puns that hide the meaning.

## 4. Body copy

- Talk to "you" more than about "we". Translate every feature into what it does for the reader.
- Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences), plain words, active voice. Aim for easy reading (roughly a 12-year-old's reading level) without being childish.
- Make it scannable: meaningful subheadings, bullets for lists of three or more, bold only for the few phrases a skimmer must catch.
- Address objections where they arise: price near pricing, effort near onboarding, risk near the CTA.
- End every section with a reason to continue or a way to act.

## 5. Calls to action and microcopy

- **CTA labels:** verb + value, from the visitor's point of view: "Get my free quote", "Book a 15-minute call", "Start free trial", "See available dates". Never "Submit", "Click here", or a lone "Learn more" for the primary action.
- **One primary CTA wording** across the site, so it becomes familiar. Secondary CTAs offer a lower-commitment step ("See how it works", "View pricing").
- **Risk reducers right under the CTA:** "Free and without obligation", "No credit card needed", "Reply within one working day", "Free returns for 30 days".
- **Form microcopy:** say why you ask for sensitive data; hint at format with examples; error messages say how to fix the problem.
- **Empty and error states** speak like a helpful person: what happened, what to do next.

## 6. Proof and claims

- Testimonials: a real name, role or place, photo if allowed, and a specific result ("Back on the pitch after 5 weeks"). Edit for length, never for meaning.
- Ratings with their source and count ("4.8 on Google from 214 reviews"), linked where possible.
- Numbers: concrete and current ("1,200 homes cleaned since 2019").
- Never invent reviews, clients, numbers, certifications, awards or scarcity ("only 2 left!") that aren't real. It's dishonest, often illegal (consumer protection rules), and it destroys trust when spotted.
- Superlatives ("best", "#1", "fastest") only with a source. Health, finance and legal claims need extra care; when in doubt, describe what you do rather than promise outcomes.

## 7. Search-aware writing

- Each page targets one primary keyword cluster from seo-plan.md. Use the primary phrase naturally in the title, H1 (or close to it), the first paragraph, and at least one subheading. Variants and related questions go in H2s, body copy and the FAQ.
- Answer the questions people actually search for, clearly and early in the section (answer first, then detail). This serves featured snippets, AI answers and impatient humans alike.
- Write for people first: if a sentence reads awkwardly because of a keyword, rewrite it. Repetition and stuffing hurt.
- Unique copy for every page. Don't spin the same text across city pages or product variants.
- Meaningful link text ("see our sports injury treatment", not "click here") for internal links.

## 8. Languages and localisation

- Write natively in each language rather than translating literally. Idioms, sentence length and persuasion norms differ.
- Use the form of address decided in discovery (formal/informal) consistently.
- Localise formats: currency, dates, phone numbers, addresses, units, decimal separators.
- Keyword research is per language and market; the best keyword in one language may not be the literal translation.
- Leave room in the layout: German and Dutch run about 20–35% longer than English; buttons and navigation must survive it.

## 9. Interface copy (panels, shops)

- Labels are nouns or short verb phrases ("Invoices", "Create invoice"), consistent across the product.
- Buttons describe the action ("Save changes", "Send invoice", "Delete 3 users"), not "OK".
- Destructive confirmations name the object and the consequence ("Delete project 'Spring launch'? This removes 42 tasks and can't be undone.").
- Status text is specific ("Saved just now", "Syncing 12 of 40…").
- Shop microcopy reduces purchase anxiety: delivery estimate, return policy, secure payment, stock status, all near the add-to-cart button.
